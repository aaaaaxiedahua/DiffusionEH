\begin{abstract}
Knowledge graph reasoning infers missing facts by aggregating query-relevant evidence over relational structures. An important challenge is to use relational context to coordinate query-relevant message formation and the integration of evidence reaching the same candidate entity. To address this challenge, we propose RPCD-GNN, a progressive reasoning framework that integrates query-conditioned relational control with phase-aware entity reasoning. A relational controller propagates relation states over a fixed, sparsified weighted relation-co-occurrence graph to generate layer-wise relational contexts. These contexts jointly condition message content, admission gates, and interaction phases. A complex-domain operator derives real, imaginary, and coherent-amplitude statistics from gated incoming messages, and fuses their projected response with direct message accumulation to model phase-dependent interactions while retaining additive support. The fused response recurrently updates candidate entity states for answer prediction. Experiments on five benchmark datasets show that RPCD-GNN achieves competitive performance against representative knowledge graph reasoning methods.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Knowledge graphs (KGs) represent real-world facts as relational triples connecting entities through semantic relations. Since real-world KGs are incomplete, knowledge graph reasoning seeks to infer missing facts from observed relational structures, supporting applications such as question answering, recommender systems, and drug discovery \citep{Ji2022Survey,Cao2019KGRec,Zeng2022DrugDiscovery}. This work focuses on entity prediction queries of the form $(h_q,r_q,?)$, where the answer entity is inferred from the query entity, query relation, and available graph evidence \citep{QueryPerspectiveSurvey2025}. Effective multi-hop reasoning requires both identifying query-relevant evidence and integrating messages that reach the same candidate entity.

Existing approaches can be broadly grouped into embedding-based, path- or rule-based, and graph neural network (GNN)-based methods. Embedding methods learn compact representations for triple scoring \citep{Bordes2013TransE,Trouillon2016ComplEx,Sun2019RotatE}, while path- and rule-based methods use relational sequences or logical rules to expose explicit reasoning evidence \citep{Xiong2017DeepPath,Sadeghian2019DRUM}. GNN-based models encode relational neighborhoods through message passing, and query-conditioned variants support multi-hop inference by propagating query-specific entity states.

Query-conditioned graph reasoners have developed along several complementary directions, including query-dependent state propagation, adaptive propagation selection, and richer message representations. NBFNet and RED-GNN support query-dependent propagation and state updates \citep{Zhu2021NBFNet,Zhang2022REDGNN}, while AdaProp and Top-p reasoning adapt the propagation range \citep{Zhang2023AdaProp,Liu2025TopPReasoning}. DiffusionE, HyperKGR, and QAPD explore semantic guidance, hyperbolic representations, and query-conditioned edge competition, respectively \citep{Cao2024DiffusionE,HyperKGR2025,QAPD2026}.

Relation-centric reasoning provides another perspective on graph evidence by modeling structural associations among relations. Candidate-local relational contexts, relation-type graphs, and query-conditioned relation representations provide context for entity-level inference \citep{Geng2023RMPI,Lee2023InGram,Galkin2024ULTRA,Du2026GraphOracle}. More recent studies examine relation motifs and rule-derived patterns \citep{MOTIF2025,AdaRPT2025,MPRG2026}.

The central issue considered here is how to coordinate relation-level context with the integration of messages converging on the same candidate entity. At the edge level, relational context can guide message content, admission strength, and participation in aggregation. At the candidate level, an explicit interaction statistic can complement direct message accumulation. We investigate a unified mechanism that connects query-conditioned relational control with cross-message aggregation.

To this end, we propose RPCD-GNN, a knowledge graph reasoning framework that integrates query-conditioned relational control with phase-aware message aggregation. A relational controller generates layer-wise contexts over a fixed, sparsified weighted relation-co-occurrence graph, jointly conditioning message content, admission gates, and interaction phases. The aggregation operator combines real and imaginary message aggregates with their coherent amplitude, and fuses their projected response with direct message accumulation to recurrently update candidate entity states for answer ranking.

The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
\item We develop a query-conditioned relational controller that generates layer-wise contexts over a sparsified weighted relation-co-occurrence graph. These contexts jointly parameterize message content, admission gates, and interaction phases, enabling relation-level structural information to guide message formation and aggregation.
\item We introduce a complex-domain message-interaction operator that combines real and imaginary aggregates with their coherent amplitude, and fuses their projected response with direct message accumulation. The coherent amplitude captures relative-phase-dependent interactions among messages converging on the same candidate, while direct accumulation preserves additive message support.
\item We conduct experiments on five benchmark datasets to evaluate RPCD-GNN against representative reasoning methods. The results demonstrate competitive reasoning performance, and ablation studies further support the effectiveness of the relational controller and phase-aware message aggregation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{Embedding-, Path-, and Rule-based Knowledge Graph Reasoning}\label{subsec:embedding-path-reasoning}

Embedding-based methods represent entities and relations in continuous spaces and evaluate candidate triples through parameterized scoring functions. Translational models, represented by TransE and TransR, model relations as transformations between entity embeddings, whereas semantic matching methods such as DistMult and TuckER capture relational compatibility through bilinear or tensor operations \citep{Bordes2013TransE,Lin2015TransR,Yang2015DistMult,Balazevic2019TuckER}. Neural scoring models, including ConvE and InteractE, further enhance feature interactions with convolutional architectures \citep{Dettmers2018ConvE,Vashishth2020InteractE}. These methods provide efficient triple-level prediction but do not explicitly expose the multi-hop evidence supporting a candidate answer.

Path- and rule-based methods instead reason through explicit relational compositions. PTransE incorporates relation paths into representation learning, while Path-RNN, DeepPath, and MINERVA encode relational paths or learn policies for traversing the graph \citep{Lin2015PTransE,Das2017PathRNN,Xiong2017DeepPath,Das2018MINERVA}. DRUM learns differentiable logical rules, whereas PathCon combines relational context with paths connecting an entity pair for relation prediction \citep{Sadeghian2019DRUM,Wang2021PathCon}. Their emphasis on explicit relational compositions provides a complementary perspective to neighborhood-based evidence aggregation.

Recent work also connects logical rules with neural propagation. Rule-Guided GNNs incorporate rule guidance into model training and examine the confidence of rules extracted from trained networks \citep{RuleGuidedGNN2025}. This line emphasizes explicit logical constraints and rule-level explanations in graph-based reasoning.

\subsection{Graph-based Knowledge Graph Reasoning}\label{subsec:progressive-gnn-reasoning}

Relational GNNs encode multi-relational neighborhoods through message passing. R-GCN applies relation-specific transformations during neighborhood aggregation, whereas CompGCN jointly composes entity and relation representations \citep{Schlichtkrull2018RGCN,Vashishth2020CompGCN}. Recent relational encoders further refine neighborhood information: AdaGCN adaptively aggregates or separates neighbor features \citep{AdaGCN2025}, while HIGSM combines hierarchical and inter-layer encoding in a modular graph self-attention architecture \citep{HIGSM2025}.

Query-conditioned reasoners use graph propagation to integrate multi-hop evidence for each query. NBFNet formulates link prediction through generalized Bellman--Ford reasoning, and RED-GNN recursively encodes query-dependent relational subgraphs \citep{Zhu2021NBFNet,Zhang2022REDGNN}. AdaProp learns query-adaptive propagation structures, while A*Net uses a learned query-conditioned priority function to explore selected nodes and edges \citep{Zhang2023AdaProp,Zhu2023AStarNet}. Top-p reasoning adapts the layer-wise propagation range through multi-layer Top-p message passing \citep{Liu2025TopPReasoning}. DiffusionE interprets entity-state propagation as a semantics-guided diffusion process \citep{Cao2024DiffusionE}.

Query-dependent GNN reasoning has also advanced in representation geometry and propagation conditioning. HyperKGR embeds recursive reasoning trees in hyperbolic space to obtain query-specific representations \citep{HyperKGR2025}. QAPD introduces query-conditioned edge competition, adaptive cross-layer fusion, and relation-pattern-aware decoding for entity-inductive reasoning \citep{QAPD2026}.

Beyond progressive GNNs, KnowFormer explores structure-aware Transformer attention and relational message passing as an alternative paradigm for knowledge graph reasoning \citep{Liu2024KnowFormer}. Together, these studies examine how relational encoding, propagation selection, representation geometry, and attention mechanisms shape query-dependent graph reasoning.

\subsection{Relation-centric Structural Modeling}\label{subsec:relation-centric-modeling}

Relation-centric reasoning methods operate at different structural granularities. ConGLR augments a candidate-specific enclosing subgraph with a heterogeneous context graph for inductive relation prediction, whereas RMPI transforms a candidate-local subgraph into a line-graph-style relation-instance view and performs target-relation-guided message passing for fully inductive reasoning \citep{Lin2022ConGLR,Geng2023RMPI}. These methods construct local relational contexts for individual candidate triples rather than a graph-level topology over relation types.

Other approaches explicitly build graph-level structures among relation types. InGram derives a weighted relation graph from shared-head and shared-tail incidences and uses it to generate representations for entities and relations unseen during training \citep{Lee2023InGram}. ULTRA defines four endpoint-sharing interaction types among relations and marks the query relation to obtain query-conditioned relation representations for a subsequent entity-level GNN \citep{Galkin2024ULTRA}. GraphOracle propagates query-conditioned relation states over a directed relation-dependency graph induced by consecutive relation pairs \citep{Du2026GraphOracle}.

Recent work expands the structural information used in relation representations. MOTIF analyzes how relation motifs affect the expressive power of knowledge graph foundation models \citep{MOTIF2025}. AdaRPT combines rule-derived and co-occurrence relation features with transferable path features for entities \citep{AdaRPT2025}. MPRG constructs a relation graph from mined logical patterns and uses the resulting relation representations to guide entity-level reasoning \citep{MPRG2026}.

Transfer-oriented approaches also study how graph structure supports generalization. TRIX uses more expressive triplet representations for zero-shot entity and relation prediction \citep{TRIX2025}, while Flock encodes sampled random walks as sequences and pools node and relation representations for zero-shot link prediction \citep{Flock2026}.

These approaches differ in the relational structures they encode and the ways their representations support entity-level inference. RPCD-GNN uses layer-wise query-conditioned relation contexts as shared conditioning signals for message content, admission gates, and interaction phases, connecting relational structural modeling with message aggregation.

\subsection{Complex-Domain Representations and Message Interaction}\label{subsec:complex-message-interaction}

Complex-domain representations have been widely studied in knowledge graph embedding. ComplEx employs complex bilinear scoring to capture symmetric and asymmetric relations, RotatE represents each relation as an element-wise rotation in the complex plane, and QuatE uses quaternion embeddings and Hamilton products to model richer relational patterns \citep{Trouillon2016ComplEx,Sun2019RotatE,Zhang2019QuatE}. In these approaches, complex or hypercomplex components primarily define entity--relation transformations and triple-scoring geometry.

Complex values have also been introduced into graph neural networks. MagNet constructs a complex Hermitian magnetic Laplacian whose magnitude represents undirected graph structure and whose phase encodes edge direction, enabling complex spectral convolution over directed graphs \citep{Zhang2021MagNet}.

Attention-based GNNs such as GAT and KBGAT compute edge-wise compatibility scores, normalize them over a neighborhood, and aggregate the corresponding messages through a weighted sum \citep{Velickovic2018GAT,Nathani2019KBGAT}. Neighborhood normalization couples the attention coefficients through competition.

RPCD-GNN learns a phase for each gated message from the source state, relational context, and query relation. The resulting real and imaginary aggregates and coherent amplitude form intermediate statistics for message aggregation. The coherent amplitude captures relative-phase-dependent cross terms among messages reaching the same candidate. The projected response of all three statistics is fused with direct message accumulation, connecting context-dependent phase interactions with additive message support.

\section{Methodology}\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Notation}\label{subsec:problem-formulation}

Let $\mathcal{G}_{g}=(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{T}_{g})$ denote the observed fact graph available for reasoning, where $\mathcal{E}$, $\mathcal{R}$, and $\mathcal{T}_{g}$ are the entity set, relation set, and observed triple set, respectively. Each triple $(h,r,t)\in\mathcal{T}_{g}$ states that head entity $h$ is connected to tail entity $t$ by relation $r$. Given a query pair $q=(h_q,r_q)$ corresponding to the incomplete triple $(h_q,r_q,?)$, the task is to score all candidate entities and return
\begin{equation}
\hat{t}
=
\arg\max_{e\in\mathcal{E}}
score(h_q,r_q,e).
\label{eq:reasoning-task}
\end{equation}

Let $\mathcal{T}_{f}$ denote the fixed fact set used to construct relation-co-occurrence associations. The resulting relation topology is shared across queries, while $\mathcal{T}_{g}$ specifies the facts available for entity propagation under each experimental protocol. These graph constructions are detailed in Section~\ref{subsec:implementation-details}.

Entity states, messages, and relation representations lie in $\mathbb{R}^{d}$; relation-attention, admission, and phase encoders use hidden width $a$. The query vector $\mathbf{r}_{q}^{(l)}=\mathbf{r}_{r_q}^{(l)}$ is the layer-specific base embedding of the query relation. Reasoning starts from $\mathcal{V}^{(0)}=\{h_q\}$ with $\mathbf{h}_{h_q}^{(0,q)}=\mathbf{0}$. Base relation embeddings and entity-level message, admission, phase, and selection parameters are layer-specific; relational-controller and GRU parameters are shared across reasoning layers. Layer indices on trainable parameters and bias terms are omitted in the equations for readability.

We use ReLU in the relation-attention, message, admission, and phase encoders. The symbol $\phi$ denotes the dataset-specific post-aggregation activation, while sigmoid and $\tanh$ are used for admission and phase assignment. Temperatures are positive, $\epsilon>0$ denotes a numerical stabilizer, and $\beta,\lambda\in[0,1]$ control relation-state mixing. The main notation is summarized in Table~\ref{tbl:notations}.

\subsection{Model Overview}\label{subsec:overall-framework}

Figure~\ref{fig:framework} presents RPCD-GNN as a relational controller coupled with phase-aware entity reasoning. Given $q=(h_q,r_q)$, the query entity initializes entity propagation and the query relation conditions the controller. At layer $l$, the controller generates $\widetilde{\mathbf{R}}^{(l,q)}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{R}'|\times d}$ by stacking the fused relation states. This context jointly controls message content, admission gates, and interaction phases.

Messages reaching the same candidate are summarized by direct accumulation and complex-domain statistics. Their projected responses are fused into a provisional candidate state, followed by candidate retention and a recurrent state update. The process repeats for $L$ layers, with relational contexts recomputed at each layer, and the final states are scored for answer ranking. The figure uses $h$ and $r$ for $h_q$ and $r_q$. Sections~\ref{subsec:relation-codiffusion}--\ref{subsec:model-training} define the component computations. For readability, the query superscript is omitted below when unambiguous.

\subsection{Query-Conditioned Relational Context}\label{subsec:relation-codiffusion}

The effective relation identifier space is $\mathcal{R}'=\mathcal{R}\cup\mathcal{R}^{-1}\cup\{r_{\mathrm{id}}\}$. The entity propagation graph is augmented with inverse edges and identity self-loops. RPCD-GNN constructs a sparsified weighted relation-co-occurrence graph $\mathcal{G}_{r}=(\mathcal{R}',\mathcal{E}_{r})$ from the fixed facts $\mathcal{T}_{f}$. Inverse relation incidences are included in this construction according to the reported configuration. The topology and structural weights remain fixed, while relation states are conditioned on the query at each reasoning layer.

Let $\mathcal{A}_e$ denote the multiset of direction-aware relation incidences at entity $e$ induced by $\mathcal{T}_{f}$, where inverse types distinguish incoming from outgoing incidences and $n_e(r)$ records the multiplicity of relation $r$. Associations are counted between relation types sharing an anchor entity with more than one incidence:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
C_{ij}
&=
\sum_{\substack{e\in\mathcal{E}\\|\mathcal{A}_e|>1}}
n_e(r_i)n_e(r_j),\\
\bar{\pi}_{ij}
&=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{C_{ij}}{\sum_k C_{ik}},
& \sum_k C_{ik}>0,\\[5pt]
0,
& \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:relation-structural-prior}
\end{equation}
The normalized co-occurrence associations are filtered by a minimum-weight threshold $\delta_r$, after which Top-$K_r$ sparsification retains the highest-weight structural associations for each receiving relation:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}_{i}
=
\operatorname{TopK}_{K_r}
\left(
\left\{
j\mid j\neq i,\ \bar{\pi}_{ij}\geq\delta_r
\right\};
\bar{\pi}_{ij}
\right).
\label{eq:relation-neighborhood}
\end{equation}
An identity self-loop is included for every effective relation type, so $\mathcal{E}_{r}$ contains the selected pairs $(r_i,r_j)$ with $j\in\mathcal{S}_{i}$ together with $(r_i,r_i)$ for each $r_i\in\mathcal{R}'$. The corresponding edge weight is
\begin{equation}
\omega_{ij}
=
\begin{cases}
1,
& i=j,\\
\bar{\pi}_{ij},
& j\in\mathcal{S}_{i},\ i\neq j.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:relation-edge-weight}
\end{equation}
The normalized weights $\bar{\pi}_{ij}$ describe statistical associations between relation incidences and do not imply logical entailment. The receiving neighborhood is $\mathcal{N}_{r}(i)=\mathcal{S}_{i}\cup\{i\}$, including the selected structural neighbors and the identity self-loop.

At entity reasoning layer $l$, relation propagation starts from the layer-specific base representations, $\mathbf{r}_{i}^{(l,0,q)}=\mathbf{r}_{i}^{(l)}$. For inner step $s=1,\ldots,J$ and relation edge $(r_i,r_j)\in\mathcal{E}_{r}$, the query-conditioned activation is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
a_{ij}^{(l,s,q)}
={}&
\mathbf{w}_{r}^{\top}\operatorname{ReLU}\bigl(
W_{s}^{r}\mathbf{r}_{i}^{(l,s-1,q)}
+W_{d}^{r}\mathbf{r}_{j}^{(l,s-1,q)}\\
&\quad+W_{q}^{r}\mathbf{r}_{q}^{(l)}
+\mathbf{u}_{\omega}\log(\omega_{ij}+\epsilon)
\bigr).
\end{split}
\label{eq:query-relation-score}
\end{equation}
Under this row-update convention, relation $r_i$ receives a message from relation $r_j$, where $r_j$ supplies the transformed neighbor state. The activations are normalized over the retained neighborhood of the receiving relation $r_i$:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{ij}^{(l,s,q)}
=
\frac{
\exp\left(a_{ij}^{(l,s,q)}/\tau_r\right)
}{
\sum_{k\in\mathcal{N}_{r}(i)}
\exp\left(a_{ik}^{(l,s,q)}/\tau_r\right)
}.
\label{eq:query-relation-activation}
\end{equation}
The relation state is propagated while retaining the base semantics:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{r}_{i}^{(l,s,q)}
=
\beta\mathbf{r}_{i}^{(l)}
+
(1-\beta)
\phi\left(
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{r}(i)}
\alpha_{ij}^{(l,s,q)}
W_{m}^{r}\mathbf{r}_{j}^{(l,s-1,q)}
\right).
\label{eq:relation-state-update}
\end{equation}
After $J$ inner steps, the query-conditioned state is fused with the base relation representation:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\mathbf{r}}_{i}^{(l,q)}
=
(1-\lambda)\mathbf{r}_{i}^{(l)}
+
\lambda\mathbf{r}_{i}^{(l,J,q)}.
\label{eq:fused-relational-context}
\end{equation}
Here, $\beta$ retains a contribution from the base relation state during inner propagation, while $\lambda$ controls its final mixture with the query-conditioned state.
The fused context $\tilde{\mathbf{r}}_{i}^{(l,q)}$ supplies a shared conditioning signal for message content, admission gating, and phase assignment in the subsequent entity update.

Figure~\ref{fig:relation-codiffusion} illustrates successive inner steps: $r_3$ receives messages from $r_1$ and $r_2$ through $\alpha_{31}$ and $\alpha_{32}$, and its updated state contributes to $r_5$ through $\alpha_{53}$. The indices in $\alpha_{ij}$ denote receiving relation $r_i$ and sending relation $r_j$; other superscripts are omitted for readability. The highlighted routes are schematic, while each update aggregates all retained neighbors for the configured $J$ steps.

\subsection{Query-Conditioned Message Formation}\label{subsec:entity-diffusion}

At layer $l$, RPCD-GNN expands the outgoing edges of the retained entity set $\mathcal{V}^{(l-1)}$ in the augmented entity propagation graph. The expanded edges form $\mathcal{E}^{(l)}$, and their targets constitute the provisional set $\overline{\mathcal{V}}^{(l)}$. Newly reached candidates are $\mathcal{C}^{(l)}=\overline{\mathcal{V}}^{(l)}\setminus\mathcal{V}^{(l-1)}$. An entity discarded earlier may re-enter if reached again. For a provisional target $v$, let $\mathcal{E}_{v}^{(l)}$ denote its incoming expanded edges. The message on $(u,r,v)$ uses a query-aware difference composition \citep{Cao2024DiffusionE}:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{m}_{u,r,v}^{(l)}
=
\operatorname{ReLU}\left(
W_{c}^{m}
\left(
\mathbf{h}_{u}^{(l-1)}
+
\tilde{\mathbf{r}}_{r}^{(l)}
\right)
-
W_{q}^{m}
\left(
\mathbf{h}_{u}^{(l-1)}
+
\mathbf{r}_{q}^{(l)}
\right)
\right).
\label{eq:semantic-evidence-message}
\end{equation}
This difference-based composition combines propagation-relation-conditioned and query-conditioned source features through separate learned projections. The relational context participates directly in message construction. An admission gate controls the strength of the resulting message:
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{u,r,v}^{(l)}
=
\operatorname{sigmoid}\left(
\mathbf{w}_{e}^{\top}
\operatorname{ReLU}\left(
W_{h}^{e}\mathbf{h}_{u}^{(l-1)}
+
W_{r}^{e}\tilde{\mathbf{r}}_{r}^{(l)}
+
W_{q}^{e}\mathbf{r}_{q}^{(l)}
\right)
\right).
\label{eq:evidence-admission-gate}
\end{equation}
The gated message is then
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{x}_{u,r,v}^{(l)}
=
\gamma_{u,r,v}^{(l)}
\mathbf{m}_{u,r,v}^{(l)}.
\label{eq:weighted-relation-evidence}
\end{equation}
Here, $\gamma_{u,r,v}^{(l)}$ controls how much message content is admitted into subsequent interaction. Since $\mathbf{m}_{u,r,v}^{(l)}$ is produced by ReLU and $\gamma_{u,r,v}^{(l)}\in(0,1)$, the gated message $\mathbf{x}_{u,r,v}^{(l)}$ is element-wise nonnegative.

\subsection{Phase-Aware Message Aggregation}\label{subsec:phase-interference}

\subsubsection{Phase Encoding and Message Aggregation}

For each gated message, we learn an interaction phase from the source state, relational context, and query relation:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\theta_{u,r}^{(l)}
={}&
\pi\tanh\left(
\frac{
\mathbf{w}_{p}^{\top}
\operatorname{ReLU}\left(
W_{p}\left[
\mathbf{h}_{u}^{(l-1)};
\tilde{\mathbf{r}}_{r}^{(l)};
\mathbf{r}_{q}^{(l)}
\right]
\right)
}{
\tau_p
}
\right).
\end{split}
\label{eq:evidence-phase}
\end{equation}
The resulting phase-encoded message is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{z}_{u,r,v}^{(l)}
=
\mathbf{x}_{u,r,v}^{(l)}
\exp\left(
\mathrm{i}\theta_{u,r}^{(l)}
\right).
\label{eq:complex-relation-phase-evidence}
\end{equation}
Here, $\theta_{u,r}^{(l)}$ is a scalar phase assigned to each edge-level message and broadcast across its hidden dimensions, with $\theta_{u,r}^{(l)}\in(-\pi,\pi)$. The complex notation is used as an intermediate interaction representation; entity states, relation states, recurrent updates, and answer scores remain real-valued.
The gated content $\mathbf{x}$ and phase $\theta$ share the same source-state, relational-context, and query-relation inputs. The content carries the admitted message features, while the phase parameterizes their interaction during aggregation.

Let $\mathcal{X}_{v}^{(l)}$ collect the gated messages and phases carried by all edges in $\mathcal{E}_{v}^{(l)}$. The direct message aggregate retains the accumulated real-valued message content:
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{v}^{(l)}
=
\sum_{(u,r,v)\in\mathcal{E}_{v}^{(l)}}
\mathbf{x}_{u,r,v}^{(l)}.
\label{eq:direct-evidence-moment}
\end{equation}
The phase-coherent aggregate is computed through real and imaginary components, together with a numerically stabilized element-wise amplitude:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{v}^{(l)}
&=\sum_{(u,r,v)\in\mathcal{E}_{v}^{(l)}}\mathbf{z}_{u,r,v}^{(l)}\\
&=\mathbf{M}_{v}^{re,(l)}+\mathrm{i}\mathbf{M}_{v}^{im,(l)},\\
\mathbf{M}_{v}^{re,(l)}
&=\sum_{(u,r,v)\in\mathcal{E}_{v}^{(l)}}
\mathbf{x}_{u,r,v}^{(l)}\cos\theta_{u,r}^{(l)},\\
\mathbf{M}_{v}^{im,(l)}
&=\sum_{(u,r,v)\in\mathcal{E}_{v}^{(l)}}
\mathbf{x}_{u,r,v}^{(l)}\sin\theta_{u,r}^{(l)},\\
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{v}^{(l)}
&=\sqrt{\left|\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{v}^{(l)}\right|^2+\epsilon}\\
&=\sqrt{\left(\mathbf{M}_{v}^{re,(l)}\right)^2
+\left(\mathbf{M}_{v}^{im,(l)}\right)^2+\epsilon}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:coherent-evidence-moment}
\end{equation}
The modulus, square, and square root in Eq.~\eqref{eq:coherent-evidence-moment} act element-wise. The stabilizer regularizes amplitude computation; the following identity concerns the unstabilized squared modulus.

Index incoming edges by $i$, with gated-message component $x_{i,k}$ and phase $\theta_i$. For hidden coordinate $k$, the coherent aggregate satisfies
\begin{equation}
\left|\kappa_{v,k}^{(l)}\right|^2
=
\sum_i x_{i,k}^{2}
+
2\sum_{i<j}
x_{i,k}x_{j,k}
\cos\left(\theta_i-\theta_j\right).
\label{eq:phase-dependent-interaction}
\end{equation}
The cross terms depend on relative phase. Aligned messages contribute positively to the coherent magnitude, whereas opposing phases can reduce the contribution of their pairwise interaction. Unlike direct additive aggregation, coherent aggregation therefore incorporates phase-dependent interactions without explicitly enumerating message pairs.

The two message aggregates are integrated by a unified phase-aware message aggregation operator:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{phase}}
\left(\mathcal{X}_{v}^{(l)}\right)
=\;&
\phi\left(
W_{\mu}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{v}^{(l)}
\right)\\
&+
\eta
\phi\left(
W_{re}\operatorname{Re}
\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{v}^{(l)}
+
W_{im}\operatorname{Im}
\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{v}^{(l)}
+
W_{\rho}\boldsymbol{\rho}_{v}^{(l)}
\right).
\end{split}
\label{eq:unified-relation-phase-operator}
\end{equation}
The direct aggregate retains additive message content, while the real, imaginary, and coherent-amplitude statistics encode phase-dependent aggregation information. Their projected responses are fused to form the provisional candidate state.

\subsubsection{Candidate Retention and Recurrent State Update}

Before the recurrent update, RPCD-GNN bounds the number of newly retained entities. The provisional state is $\widehat{\mathbf{h}}_{v}^{(l)}=\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{phase}}(\mathcal{X}_{v}^{(l)})$. Each new candidate is scored by
\begin{equation}
s_{v}^{(l)}
=
\mathbf{w}_{s}^{\top}
\widehat{\mathbf{h}}_{v}^{(l)}.
\label{eq:candidate-selection-score}
\end{equation}
Let $k_l=\min(K_l,|\mathcal{C}^{(l)}|)$. The retained set preserves previously retained entities and adds up to $K_l$ new candidates:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{V}^{(l)}
=
\mathcal{V}^{(l-1)}
\cup
\operatorname{TopK}_{k_l}
\left(
\mathcal{C}^{(l)};
\widetilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(l)}
\right).
\label{eq:topk-frontier-update}
\end{equation}
The message-derived response then updates the retained entity state:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_{v}^{(l)}
=
\operatorname{GRU}\left(
\widehat{\mathbf{h}}_{v}^{(l)},
\mathbf{h}_{v}^{(l-1)}
\right),
\quad
v\in\mathcal{V}^{(l)}.
\label{eq:recurrent-entity-update}
\end{equation}
For a nonempty candidate set, training uses Gumbel--Softmax probabilities $\widetilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(l)}=\operatorname{softmax}((\mathbf{s}^{(l)}+\mathbf{g})/\tau)$ over $\mathcal{C}^{(l)}$, where the components of $\mathbf{g}$ are independent standard Gumbel perturbations. A straight-through mask uses the binary Top-$k_l$ selection in the forward pass and the relaxed probabilities to approximate its gradient. At inference, $\widetilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(l)}=\mathbf{s}^{(l)}$ gives deterministic Top-$k_l$ selection. If $\mathcal{C}^{(l)}$ is empty, no new entity is added.

Newly retained entities have zero previous states. The cumulative retained set satisfies $|\mathcal{V}^{(l)}|\leq 1+\sum_{t=1}^{l}K_t$ and supplies the source entities for expansion at layer $l+1$.

\subsection{Answer Prediction and Model Optimization}\label{subsec:model-training}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:rpcd-forward} summarizes the query-level forward process. The fixed relation-co-occurrence structure is activated by the query relation at every reasoning layer.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Query-conditioned relation propagation and phase-aware message aggregation in RPCD-GNN}
\label{alg:rpcd-forward}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Reasoning graph $\mathcal{T}_{g}$, sparsified relation-co-occurrence graph $\mathcal{G}_{r}$, query $(h_q,r_q,?)$, layers $L$, relation steps $J$, retention budgets $\{K_l\}_{l=1}^{L}$
\Ensure Scores over $\mathcal{E}$
\State Initialize $\mathcal{V}^{(0)}=\{h_q\}$ and $\mathbf{h}_{h_q}^{(0,q)}=\mathbf{0}$
\For{$l=1$ to $L$}
    \State Initialize $\mathbf{r}_{i}^{(l,0,q)}=\mathbf{r}_{i}^{(l)}$ and propagate $\widetilde{\mathbf{R}}^{(l,q)}$ for $J$ steps
    \State Expand $\mathcal{E}^{(l)}$ and obtain $\overline{\mathcal{V}}^{(l)}$ and $\mathcal{C}^{(l)}$
    \For{each $(u,r,v)\in\mathcal{E}^{(l)}$}
        \State Form gated messages $\mathbf{x}_{u,r,v}^{(l)}$
        \State Assign $\theta_{u,r}^{(l)}$ and form $\mathbf{z}_{u,r,v}^{(l)}$
    \EndFor
    \For{each $v\in\overline{\mathcal{V}}^{(l)}$}
        \State Compute direct and phase-coherent message aggregates
        \State Form provisional state $\widehat{\mathbf{h}}_{v}^{(l)}$
    \EndFor
    \State Score $\mathcal{C}^{(l)}$, obtain $\widetilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(l)}$, and retain entities by Eq.~\eqref{eq:topk-frontier-update}
    \For{each $v\in\mathcal{V}^{(l)}$}
        \State Evolve retained state $\mathbf{h}_{v}^{(l)}$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:recurrent-entity-update}
    \EndFor
\EndFor
\State Score retained states and map logits to the global candidate space
\State \Return $\{score(h_q,r_q,e)\mid e\in\mathcal{E}\}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

After $L$ layers, retained entity states are scored and mapped to the full entity vocabulary. For batch element $b$ and entity $e$, define the global representation as
\begin{equation}
\overline{\mathbf{h}}_{b,e}^{(L)}
=
\begin{cases}
\mathbf{h}_{b,e}^{(L)}, & e\in\mathcal{V}_{b}^{(L)},\\
\mathbf{0}, & e\notin\mathcal{V}_{b}^{(L)}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:global-candidate-representation}
\end{equation}
For a single query, omitting the batch index, the answer score is
\begin{equation}
score(h_q,r_q,e)
=
\mathbf{w}_{o}^{\top}
\overline{\mathbf{h}}_{e}^{(L)}.
\label{eq:global-candidate-score}
\end{equation}
The resulting score vector is optimized with a full-entity softmax loss. For each observed training triple $(h_q,r_q,t)$, the observed tail $t$ is used as the target:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}
=
-
\log
\frac{
\exp\left(score(h_q,r_q,t)\right)
}{
\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}
\exp\left(score(h_q,r_q,e)\right)
}.
\label{eq:training-objective}
\end{equation}
At evaluation time, all entities are ranked by these scores under the filtered protocol.

\subsection{Computational Complexity}\label{subsec:computational-complexity}

Let $B$ be the query batch size, $d$ the hidden dimension, $a$ the attention width, and $M_R$ the number of relation-graph edges. At layer $l$, let $M_l$, $N_l$, and $C_l$ count expanded entity edges, provisional entities, and new candidates, respectively, summed over the batch. Given the expanded neighborhoods, the forward cost of relation propagation, entity computation, candidate retention, and the full-entity objective is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{O}\!\Bigg(
\sum_{l=1}^{L}
\Biggl[
{}&BJM_R(d^2+da)
+M_l(d^2+da)\\
{}&+N_ld^2
+C_l\log(1+K_l)
\Biggr]\\
{}&+B|\mathcal{E}|
\Bigg).
\end{split}
\label{eq:computational-complexity}
\end{equation}
The relation term covers $J$ inner propagation steps. Entity-edge computation includes message content, admission, and phase assignment, while the node term covers aggregate projections and recurrent updates. The retention bound assumes heap-based Top-$K_l$ selection over the current candidate set; the logarithm is written as $\log(1+K_l)$ to include $K_l=1$. The final term accounts for assembling full-entity scores and evaluating the softmax objective. This bound excludes graph retrieval and indexing costs, which depend on the storage structure. Dense entity-vocabulary selection instead processes $B|\mathcal{E}|$ entries per layer and requires corresponding selection work and buffers.

Since each receiving relation has at most $K_r$ structural neighbors and one identity self-loop, $M_R\leq|\mathcal{R}'|(K_r+1)$. Relation-context computation is therefore bounded by $\mathcal{O}(BLJ|\mathcal{R}'|(K_r+1)(d^2+da))$ after preprocessing. Co-occurrence counting costs $\mathcal{O}(\sum_e c_e^2)$, where $c_e=|\mathcal{A}_e|$. If $D_i$ denotes the number of nonzero associations in row $i$, normalization and heap-based sparsification add $\mathcal{O}(\sum_i D_i[1+\log(1+K_r)])$. These preprocessing steps are performed once.

Excluding trainable parameters, persistent relation storage is $\mathcal{O}(M_R+B|\mathcal{R}'|d)$ for topology, weights, and query-conditioned states. A vectorized relation step additionally uses edge-wise activations of order $\mathcal{O}(BM_R(d+a))$. Entity-message and phase computations use $\mathcal{O}(M_l(d+a)+N_ld)$ activations, and full-entity scores require $\mathcal{O}(B|\mathcal{E}|)$ storage. Inference working memory is governed by the largest active layer together with persistent storage. Training also retains activations across the $L$ entity layers and $J$ inner relation steps for backpropagation, so the relation-state storage bound alone does not describe training memory.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Datasets}\label{subsec:datasets}

We evaluate RPCD-GNN in the transductive setting, using a fixed entity and relation vocabulary across the training, validation, and test splits. Experiments are conducted on five knowledge graph reasoning datasets: Family, UMLS, WN18RR, NELL-995, and FB15k-237. These datasets span different graph scales and relation vocabularies, allowing us to examine query-conditioned relation propagation and complex-domain message interaction across compact relational benchmarks and larger knowledge graphs.

Family is a relational reasoning dataset derived from kinship relations, where the target is to infer implicit family relations from observed relational facts \citep{Kok2007Statistical,Sadeghian2019DRUM}. UMLS is a biomedical relational dataset constructed from the Unified Medical Language System and is commonly used to evaluate multi-relational reasoning over medical concepts \citep{Bodenreider2004UMLS,Sadeghian2019DRUM}. WN18RR is a leakage-reduced subset of WordNet that removes inverse relations from WN18 to reduce test leakage \citep{Dettmers2018ConvE}. FB15k-237 is derived from Freebase by removing near-duplicate and inverse relations and is a widely used benchmark for link prediction \citep{Toutanova2015Observed,Dettmers2018ConvE}. NELL-995 is a knowledge graph reasoning dataset constructed from the Never-Ending Language Learning project and has the largest entity vocabulary among the five datasets \citep{Xiong2017DeepPath}.

The released benchmark files contain a permanent fact set $\mathcal{T}_{f}$, a training set $\mathcal{T}_{tr}$, a validation set $\mathcal{T}_{va}$, and a test set $\mathcal{T}_{te}$. Their statistics are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:dataset-statistics}; the graph--query construction used at each experimental stage is detailed in Section~\ref{subsec:implementation-details}.

\subsection{Baselines and Evaluation Metrics}\label{subsec:baselines-metrics}

We compare RPCD-GNN with representative knowledge graph reasoning methods. Embedding-based baselines include ConvE \citep{Dettmers2018ConvE}, QuatE \citep{Zhang2019QuatE}, and RotatE \citep{Sun2019RotatE}; path- and rule-based baselines include MINERVA \citep{Das2018MINERVA}, DRUM \citep{Sadeghian2019DRUM}, and RNNLogic \citep{Qu2021RNNLogic}. We also include NodePiece \citep{Galkin2022NodePiece} and MorsE with RotatE as its base scoring function \citep{Chen2021MorsE}, providing comparisons with entity representation approaches designed to generalize beyond learned entity embeddings.

The GNN-based baselines include CompGCN \citep{Vashishth2020CompGCN}, NBFNet \citep{Zhu2021NBFNet}, RED-GNN \citep{Zhang2022REDGNN}, AdaProp \citep{Zhang2023AdaProp}, A*Net \citep{Zhu2023AStarNet}, DiffusionE \citep{Cao2024DiffusionE}, and Top-p reasoning \citep{Liu2025TopPReasoning}. CompGCN represents full-graph relational aggregation, whereas NBFNet, RED-GNN, AdaProp, A*Net, DiffusionE, and Top-p reasoning instantiate different forms of query-dependent progressive propagation. The comparison includes representative reasoning methods whose dataset splits and filtered-ranking protocols are compatible with our setting.

Following the standard filtered-ranking protocol, we evaluate both tail prediction and head prediction, where head-prediction queries are converted into tail-prediction queries using inverse relations. For each query, all entities are ranked according to their prediction scores, while other known correct answers in $\mathcal{T}_{f}\cup\mathcal{T}_{tr}\cup\mathcal{T}_{va}\cup\mathcal{T}_{te}$ are removed only when computing the rank of the target entity. We report mean reciprocal rank (MRR), Hits@1, and Hits@10, with higher values indicating better reasoning performance.

\subsection{Implementation Details}\label{subsec:implementation-details}

At each training epoch $s$, $\mathcal{T}_{f}$ and $\mathcal{T}_{tr}$ are pooled and randomly repartitioned according to the dataset-specific fact ratio $\varrho$. The retained triples form the current reasoning graph $\mathcal{T}_{g}^{(s)}$, while the remaining triples provide supervised queries $\mathcal{T}_{q}^{(s)}$. During validation and testing, $\mathcal{T}_{f}\cup\mathcal{T}_{tr}$ forms the fixed reasoning graph, and queries are drawn from $\mathcal{T}_{va}$ and $\mathcal{T}_{te}$, respectively. The topology and structural weights of the relation-co-occurrence graph are constructed once from $\mathcal{T}_{f}$ and remain fixed throughout training and evaluation, whereas its query-conditioned relational context is recomputed for each query and reasoning layer.

The entity propagation graph is augmented with inverse edges and identity self-loops. For FB15k-237, query-associated one-hop edges are removed from $\mathcal{T}_{g}^{(s)}$ following the transductive protocol used by DiffusionE~\citep{Cao2024DiffusionE}. The inclusion of inverse incidences in the relation-co-occurrence graph follows the reported relation-context configuration.

All experiments are conducted using Python 3.8 and PyTorch 2.3 on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU. RPCD-GNN is optimized using Adam with an exponentially decayed learning rate. The principal backbone, training, relation-propagation, and phase-aggregation settings are reported in Table~\ref{tab:implementation-settings}; values are rounded for readability. The model is trained for at most 300 epochs. RPCD-specific parameters are selected according to validation MRR, and the checkpoint with the highest validation MRR is used for final evaluation. Results for RPCD-GNN in the main comparisons, ablation studies, and parameter sensitivity analyses are averaged over five independent runs.

On Family, where leading methods already achieve near-perfect scores, RPCD-GNN ties for the best Hits@1 and remains close to the highest MRR. On NELL-995, it obtains the second-best Hits@10 of 0.657 but does not achieve the best MRR or Hits@1. Thus, the method is competitive across the evaluated benchmarks, with its clearest advantages on UMLS and WN18RR. The following ablation study examines the contributions of its two components.

The effect of the phase-aware aggregation weight $\eta$ also differs across datasets. WN18RR achieves its highest performance at $\eta=0.1$, whereas FB15k-237 favors $\eta=0.6$. Both datasets benefit from a nonzero phase-aware contribution, but increasing its weight beyond these values reduces performance. This suggests that the contribution of the phase-aware branch should be balanced with that of direct aggregation.

Figure~\ref{fig:reasoning-path-case} presents an illustrative reasoning subgraph constructed with UMLS entity and relation names for the query \texttt{(Pharmacologic\_Substance, Treats, ?)}, with \texttt{Disease\_or\_Syndrome} as a candidate answer. The example shows how query-conditioned relational context informs message computation and how phase-aware aggregation combines incoming messages at a candidate entity.

The example highlights two complementary stages of RPCD-GNN. Query-conditioned relational context is incorporated into message content, gating, and phase encoding. When messages from different branches reach the same candidate, their learned phases affect the real and imaginary aggregates. These aggregates and their stabilized amplitudes form the phase-aware branch, which is combined with direct aggregation to update the candidate state.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper, we proposed RPCD-GNN, a progressive knowledge graph reasoning framework that coordinates query-conditioned relational context with phase-aware message aggregation. A relational controller generates layer-wise contexts that jointly regulate message content, admission gates, and interaction phases. A complex-domain aggregation operator models phase-dependent interactions among messages converging on the same candidate and combines them with direct message accumulation for entity-state updating.

Experiments on five benchmark datasets demonstrate competitive link-prediction performance, with the best results among the compared methods on UMLS and WN18RR across all reported metrics. Ablation studies further show that both the relational controller and phase-aware aggregation contribute to the reported performance.

The current framework uses a fixed relation-co-occurrence graph. Future work will explore adaptive updates to this graph and the integration of textual information to enrich query-conditioned relational context.
